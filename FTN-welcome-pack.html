Getting Started

Important: To make sure our emails reach you, please add all @a2bglobalmedia.com addresses to your address book or contact list. Not sure you are receiving our emails? Please get in touch.

You build and update your company page yourself, in Manage My Page on your Future Transport-News account.

Watch our Onboarding Guide for a step-by-step tour, from your activation email to your published page.

  1. Activate your accountWe email you an invitation with the subject “Activate your account to manage your company”. Click the link within a week. You are signed straight in.
  2. Keep your login detailsA second email, “Your login details for your company”, gives your username and password. Keep it for when you next sign in. If your page appears on more than one of our sites, the same login works on each.
  3. Build your pageGo to My Account › Manage My Page and work through the sections: Company details, Company imagery, Company profile, Company categories and Company events. Click the Save button at the end of each section before you open the next one. Your page stays a private draft until it is published.
  4. Submit for publicationWhen the checklist below is complete, click Submit for publication. Our Content Team reviews your page and publishes it.

Before you can submit

  • Company name
  • Contact email
  • Website URL
  • Telephone
  • Address
  • Region
  • Logo
  • At least one hero image
  • Company profile of at least 300 words
  • At least one category

Your page stays editable after it is published, so you can keep improving it. If you are on a Premium or Ultra package, we also book an onboarding call with you.

Some things are not in Manage My Page yet: videos, documents, press releases and banners. Send these to the Content Team. For large files, use a transfer service such as:

Company Imagery

  • Logo (required): upload it and position it in the square crop. We place it on a white square so it sits neatly across the site. A PNG with a transparent background works best.
  • Hero images (at least one, up to 20): these rotate at the top of your page, in the order you set. Landscape photographs at least 1600px wide look best.
  • Library images (optional, up to 30): your products, projects and team. Give each one a caption if you would like it labelled when a visitor enlarges it. A good caption also helps your images appear in image search.

Use the best-quality images you have, in JPEG or PNG format. The best images reflect the key points of your text.

Profile Text

300–1,000 words. You write your profile, and we take care of the technical side, such as the page address, meta description and other search settings. A profile strength meter shows your word count and ticks off a heading, paragraphs, a link and a list as you add them. You need 300 words to submit, and the meter reaches full strength at 1,000.

Search engines, and AI tools such as ChatGPT and Google’s AI Overviews, read your profile to decide when to show it or quote it. These eight tips help them understand what you do, and help your readers too.

Important: Your content can be similar to what already exists elsewhere on the web, but please do not copy it word for word. Search engines penalise copied content.
  1. Start with a summary that stands on its ownOpen with two or three sentences that say who you are, what you supply, who to and where. AI tools often quote a page’s opening sentences, so they need to make sense on their own.
  2. Give every section a heading that says what it is aboutYour profile is one text box. To make a heading, click in the line and choose Heading from the Paragraph menu in the toolbar (choose Subheading for a smaller one). Each heading appears as a main heading on your page, and search engines rely on these headings to understand it. Avoid “About Us” or “Our Products”. You can also write a heading as a question your buyers ask, such as “How does Company ABC cut depot maintenance time?”
  3. Plan your sections
    • Overview: the heading includes your company name and what you do, for example “Company ABC – EV Charging Systems for Fleets and Car Parks”.
    • Applications: one section for each application you work in, for example “Fleet Depot Charging” (not just “Charging”) or “Public Charging Hubs”. Describe the products you supply for each one.
    • Case studies: give each one a precise heading, such as “Case Study: Overnight Charging for a 60-Van Delivery Fleet”, then set out the challenge, what you supplied, and the result, with a number if you can.
  4. Keep to one topic per section, in short paragraphsAim for two to four sentences per paragraph. Search engines and AI tools often pick out a single passage rather than the whole page, so each section should make sense if read on its own.
  5. Use lists for anything that is naturally a listProducts, services, sectors, technical specifications and certifications are all easier to scan as a bulleted list, and AI tools reuse lists readily.
  6. Give facts, not claimsName the standards you meet (for example OCPP 2.0.1 or ISO 15118), the projects and customers you can mention, and figures such as fleet numbers, countries or years in business. Words like “world-leading” and “innovative” add nothing unless a fact backs them up.
  7. Use the words your customers search for, and use them consistentlyBe precise rather than vague: “EV fleet charging software” is better than just “software”. Write your company name exactly as it appears in Company details, and spell product names the same way every time, so search engines recognise them.
  8. Link to your own pages with descriptive link textLink to the relevant product pages or case studies on your website. Use words that describe the page, such as “our fleet charging case studies”, rather than “click here”.
